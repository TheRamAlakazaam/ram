A big motivation for making this blog was websites like StumbleUpon (now Mix) that would introduce niche sites to you based on your interests. You would simply click the “StumbleUpon” button and suddenly find weird games, wacky websites, educational content, stories, and blogs like “Johnny’s List” or “Zen List.” Those were some of the sites I kept up with the most.

A lot of the content revolved around sharing helpful tools, ideas, and fun websites to explore. I figured it would be great to contribute to that type of community by introducing people to sites that probably don’t get as much attention anymore. It’s hard to imagine that a large part of the population rarely navigates beyond big tech platforms, news sources, and shopping websites.

I think a lot of that has to do with algorithms controlling how people experience the internet now. Most social media platforms are designed to keep you scrolling on the same few apps instead of encouraging you to explore outside of them. Eventually, the internet starts feeling smaller, even though there’s technically more content than ever.

I’ve slowly started experiencing a kind of digital isolationism where people don’t really share ideas online the same way anymore. Social media has been overtaken by slop content, and very little real interaction takes place between people. Everything has been condensed into the quick thrill of the like button.

Websites like these remind me that the internet can still feel creative, weird, useful, and human. Not everything has to be optimized for engagement, monetization, or becoming a brand.

4. [Gnoosic **-**](https://www.gnoosic.com)<strong></strong> Discover New Music (Gnoosic)
5. [Radiooooo **-**](https://app.radiooooo.com)<strong></strong> Discover music from different countries and eras.
6. [Quick Draw With Google -](https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com/) I know… it’s big tech, but it’s cool.
